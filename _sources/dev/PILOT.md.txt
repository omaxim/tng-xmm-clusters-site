# The pilot run before the freeze

Written 2026-11-10, before any pilot fit. The pilot fits a small set of FLAMINGO mock views and of real observations
with the configuration that is to be frozen, and asks the two questions the freeze depends on:

1. Do the fit's masses of real clusters agree with published XMM-Newton hydrostatic masses of the same clusters?
2. What M_fit / M_true does the same fit give on FLAMINGO mocks, and is it a hydrostatic bias of the size the
   literature expects?

The closure grid stays what it is, the test of the fitter on clusters in exact hydrostatic equilibrium. Its numbers
are quoted beside the pilot's (below); it does not answer either question.

| | list | rule |
|---|---|---|
| mocks | `campaigns/pilot_v1/pilot_mock_views.csv`, 60 views | FLAMINGO L1_m8 views of the generation of 2026-11-05 (job 1895395, commit 428a3f5, generation hash fb0a32d23c71b36f, the hash of the preset on 2026-11-10): projection 0, split `train`, z_obs inside the redshift range of the real list, spread over four bins of true M500c and over redshift |
| real | `campaigns/pilot_v1/pilot_real_obs.csv`, 60 observations | observations with a hydrostatic M500c of Lovisari et al. (2020), spread evenly in redshift; rows with no image, an excluded donor or a centre flag are left out |

`tools/make_pilot_lists.py` writes both and states the rules in full. Neither list was chosen with a fit result in
hand. The mock views carry the observing conditions they were generated with (the donor rows borrowed from the
TNG-Cluster list by the cross-simulation rule, DECISIONS "Cross-simulation mocks"), not those of the FLAMINGO target
list of 2026-11-05, which was written after them.

Fit: preset `v1` for the real observations and `v1_flamingo_l1m8` for the mocks (one `massfit` section, spec hashes
03b55819bfd0ff0d and 951c4fa187514dd9, seed hash dbcd9c755fb3a153), quick chains, the batch driver of
`slurm/fits_vera.sh` with two streams on one node. The sampler choice (PREREGISTRATION section 5) was open on
2026-11-10; the pilot uses the gate arm's sampler, so its Monte-Carlo scatter is the quick chains'.

## What is measured

A fit is inside the domain of the measurement when it carries none of the flags that are known before it runs: the
centre, a cluster that fills the field (no image has a region beyond 2 R500), a first guess limited by the field or
missing. These come from the target row and the geometry of the images, the same for mocks and real data, so the
domain does not depend on what the fit returns. Fits outside it are reported apart, as a known limit. The flags a
fit earns from its own result (prior edge, outer-pressure runaway) are different: cutting on them can remove the very
fits that disagree. Every statistic is therefore given inside the domain twice, without those fits and with them.

About half of each pilot list lies outside the domain (the clusters were not chosen by angular size), which was seen
only once the job ran: by the seed R500, 33 of the 60 real clusters and 23 of the 60 views have theta500 below 6.5
arcmin, about where the first finished fits change from flagged to clean.

* **Real.** Median of ln(M500_fit / M500_published) with a bootstrap 95 per cent interval, the scatter (1.4826 MAD),
  and the trend with redshift and with mass. The published mass is converted to the analysis cosmology as
  `obs.mass_conversion` does for every reference mass.
* **Mock, against the true mass.** Median of ln(M500_fit / M500c_true), interval, scatter, trend with mass and
  redshift.
* **Mock, against the simulation's own hydrostatic mass.** Median of ln(M500_fit / M500_HSE), where M500_HSE is the
  spherical hydrostatic mass of the truth stage from the three-dimensional gas profiles. This separates the bias of
  the gas (M_HSE / M_true, a property of the simulation) from what the X-ray measurement adds.
* **Run facts.** Core-hours per fit by stream, the longest fits, failures, QC losses: the inputs of the production
  estimate and of the speed-up work.

## How the result is read

Published numbers the result is set against (quotes and pages in PREREGISTRATION, "Published precision"):

| source | what it says |
|---|---|
| Ettori et al. (2019) | hydrostatic masses with a 3 per cent median statistical error at R500; method systematics of the order of 5 per cent |
| Pratt et al. (2019) | the hydrostatic bias of simulated clusters lies below 10 to 30 per cent |
| CHEX-MATE Collaboration (2021) | individual hydrostatic masses accurate to 15 to 20 per cent |
| closure grid, job 1896730 | this fit on clusters in exact equilibrium: 1.015 (54 clean fits), 1.046 (the 27 usable ones); 0.88 on real backgrounds (17 usable, job unfinished at the time of writing) |

Reading, as set by the author on 2026-11-10 (it replaces a first proposal of "within 10 per cent of 1", which said
nothing about the errors): a real cluster agrees with its published mass when the two 68 per cent intervals overlap,
and what is looked for is tension, not a small offset.

| question | read by | otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| real against published | tension per cluster: the fit's interval against the published one (both 68 per cent, the published errors carried to our cosmology with the mass); the report counts the clusters whose intervals do not overlap and those beyond 2 and 3 sigma, and names them. The median ratio is given with its interval, for the size of any common offset | the clusters in tension are traced one by one (conventions below first) before any freeze |
| mock against its own hydrostatic mass | the test of the measurement: same gas, same assumption, so the median M_fit / M_HSE,sim should be 1; the report says whether the interval of the median includes 1. No pull per view: the simulation's value is a point and the fit's interval is statistical only | the measurement adds a bias of its own, stated with the result |
| the simulation's hydrostatic mass against true mass | no fit involved; set against the literature | |
| mock against true mass | the product of the two above; the median M_fit / M_true lies between 0.70 and 1.00, the range of Pratt et al. (2019). On its own this cannot tell a biased fit from the simulation's bias, so it is read last | above 1: the fit is biased high on realistic gas, and the freeze waits |

No line is drawn through the tension counts: for Gaussian errors of the right size about a third of the pulls lie
beyond 1 sigma and one in twenty beyond 2. The fit's intervals come from quick chains, which the closure grid found
not converged (Rhat 1.3 to 2.2), so they may be too narrow; a pull is then too large, not too small.

### Amendment of 2026-11-11 (after 79 of the 120 fits)

Made by the author with pilot results in hand, so it is an amendment and not a rule set in advance.

The real clusters are read by three things together: no common offset inside the domain (the interval of the median
fit / published ratio includes 1), a scatter comparable to the scatter between published hydrostatic analyses of the
same clusters, and every cluster beyond 3 sigma traced and listed with what was found. Overlap of every pair of
intervals is not asked for. The reason: the statistical errors of both sides are 2 to 10 per cent, while independent
X-ray analyses of the same data differ by more than that cluster by cluster, so no pair of published analyses would
pass a test of overlap either. The size of that scatter in the literature still has to be read on the page and
entered in the ledger before the number is quoted here; until then the pilot's scatter is given without a verdict.

The sample is split by what is known before the fit. A target with a `centre`, `bg_fallback`, `field_edge` or
`no_seed` flag is outside the domain of the fit and is reported apart; inside the domain the statistics are given
once for the fits without a flag from the fit's own result and once for every finished fit.

The closure grid is reported beside the pilot as the measured error of the method on clusters in exact equilibrium.
It is no longer the gate of the freeze (PREREGISTRATION section 1, amendment of the same day).

Observation 0780291001 is excluded from the real list.

### Conventions of the published masses

The published number and ours are not the same quantity until these are accounted for. Checked on the pages of the
arXiv PDFs on 2026-11-11; the quotes in full, with the table checks, are kept with the pilot's working files
(`tngxmm_dev/PILOT/conventions/QUOTES.md`).

Which paper. The masses are those of Lovisari et al. (2020, ApJ 892, 102, arXiv 2002.11740; key `lovisari2020`),
Table A1 column (3) "M500", which Wicker et al. (2023) copy into their Table A.1 column (4), "Total mass derived
assuming hydrostatic equilibrium" (p. 24). Wicker et al. call this paper "Lovisari et al. (2020b)" ("The redshifts
and gas and total masses for the clusters were taken from Lovisari et al. (2020b)", App. A, p. 16; reference list p.
15: "2020b, ApJ, 892, 102"). Their "2020a" is the other paper of that year, Lovisari, Ettori, Sereno et al. (2020,
A&A 644, A78, arXiv 2010.03582; key `lovisari2020_massest`), which compares the same masses with other estimates and
which Wicker et al. use only for its hydrostatic-to-lensing ratio. Below, L20 is the ApJ paper and L20M the A&A one.

How these masses compare with other X-ray hydrostatic ones, from L20M: average differences "smaller than ~10%"
(abstract); 4 +- 2 per cent below Planck Collaboration (2011) with the M-YX masses (2 per cent for relaxed, 7 for
disturbed clusters), 7 +- 10 (Chandra) and 10 +- 8 (XMM) per cent below Martino et al. (2014), 9 +- 11 per cent
below Schellenberger & Reiprich (2017), about 5 and 2 per cent below the CLASH Chandra and XMM masses, and 11 per cent
below X-COP (Ettori et al. 2019), with two clusters about 30 per cent lower (p. 4, sect. 2.3; p. 7, sect. 4.1).
Their forward masses are on average 9 per cent below the backward ones, 7 per cent at z < 0.2 and 11 per cent above,
"mainly due to higher quality data of the low redshift clusters" (p. 3-4), and agree within 1 per cent for the 25
clusters with at least ten temperature bins (p. 7). Wicker et al. (2023, p. 10, sect. 5.1.1) find the same masses
below the Chandra ones of Andrade-Santos et al. (2021): log M_XMM = 0.959 log M_Chandra - 0.029, scatter 15 per cent.
L20 itself writes that different ways of computing the total mass "can easily result in an offset of 10-20%" (p. 13,
sect. 6), so a common offset of that size is a difference of method before it is an error of either.

What could make the disagreement depend on the cluster rather than be common: the concentration prior together with
the extrapolation beyond the temperature data (a cluster whose concentration is far from the Bhattacharya relation
and whose temperature profile stops short of R500 carries the prior in its published mass, not in ours), and the
depth of the temperature profile, which falls with redshift (median NT 9.5 below z = 0.2 and 5 above in the pilot
list, although fT does not fall). The cosmology conversion also depends on redshift but is done.

Transcription. The reference is L20's own Table A1 (author decision 2026-11-11), read from the arXiv LaTeX source
of the paper (`plckscaling.tex`, the longtable of the appendix) by the private literature fetcher: 120 rows, the same
113 of them in our table as before, key `lovisari2020`, reference `hse_lovisari2020_xmm` of the real fits. The parse
agrees in every row with the table read from the PDF text on the same day (M500, both errors, z, gas mass, N_T, f_T
and the N_H star). Until then the packaged rows were Wicker et al.'s Table A.1, which copies the column but differs
from L20 in 20 of the 120 clusters, all 20 in our table and 10 in the pilot list. Thirteen are truncated to one
decimal with their errors (G006.78+30.46: 17.56 +0.28 -0.27 in L20, 17.50 +- 0.20 in Wicker; G266.03-21.25:
12.56 +- 0.34 and 12.50 +- 0.30), a change of the value below 1 per cent and of the error up to 30 per cent. Seven
have lost the leading digit of the lower error: G096.87+24.21, G139.19+56.35, G229.21-17.24, G262.71-40.91,
G271.50-56.55, G280.19+47.81 and G285.63-17.24 (6.59 +1.00 -1.17 in L20, -0.17 in Wicker), all but G229.21-17.24 in
the pilot. For these the lower error was 3 to 12 times too small, so a fit below the published mass read as tension
that is not there. Wicker's rows stay in the catalogue as superseded rows and as the comparison reference
`hse_wicker2023_xmm` (`pilot_report --reference-key`).

On the 60 real fits of the pilot (all finished) the change leaves the median ratio and the scatter as they were (inside
the domain without a fit flag: 22 fits, 1.020, 0.878 to 1.185, scatter 0.32 in ln; with every ok fit: 39 fits,
0.978). It changes the tension counts. Without a fit flag, beyond 2 sigma 10 become 9 and beyond 3 sigma 7 become 5;
with every ok fit, the intervals overlap for 14 fits instead of 13, beyond 2 sigma 21 become 19 and beyond 3 sigma
14 become 12. The pulls that move are those of the lost digit: G262.73-40.92 from -3.1 to -1.2, G285.63-17.23 from
-5.4 to -2.4, G096.88+24.18 from -2.5 to -0.9 (now overlapping), G280.17+47.83 from -1.2 to -0.3; the truncated
values move G180.25+21.03 (-11.6 to -10.7), Abell 2219 and G266.04-21.25 by less than 0.2.

The report is written by `python -m tngxmm.analysis.pilot_report` (tables, the statistics above, the tension of each
fit, a figure with both error bars). It can be run while the job is going and then covers the fits finished so far.

The code is frozen on a pilot without unexplained tension on the real clusters and inside the range on the mocks.
The pilot is not the science result: 60 views of one projection are too few for a bias to two digits, and on
2026-11-10 the pairing on the fit mass had not been done (pass 1 has run since, {doc}`STATUS`).

### After the pilot: changes of 2026-11-12

- MACS J0717.5+3745 (0672420201) refitted with the corrected templates: M500 = 11.87 (11.28-12.42) e14 against
  12.91 (12.22-13.46) from Lovisari et al. in the analysis cosmology. The intervals overlap (pull -1.2); the pilot fit
  without a particle background had given 6.62 (pull -10.7). The numbers of the report above still contain the old
  fit.
- Concentration prior. With the flat prior, 11 of 28 in-domain mock fits and 7 of 39 in-domain real fits ended at a
  bound of c200. The fit now uses the concentration-mass relation of Dutton & Maccio (2014) as a prior (ledger entry
  fit.cm_prior). The reference masses of Lovisari et al. (2020) were made with the concentration fixed on a power
  law in M200 (Schellenberger & Reiprich 2017, eq. 32); ours stays free within the simulation scatter. Reweighting
  the flat-prior chains of the fits not at a bound moves M500 by a median of 0.3 per cent (real, 32 fits) and 0.0 per
  cent (mock, 17 fits), at most 10 per cent. This does not cover the 18 fits at a bound, which have to be refitted,
  and no fit has run with the new prior yet.
- The two mock fits without a result. `mock_74_80599466_v0`: the sampler could not start (no walker with a finite
  likelihood) after a 6.6 h optimisation. `mock_76_73968578_v0`: per-fit timeout (38 h) inside the optimiser. Neither
  was the disk or the time limit of the job.
- The hashes quoted in this document are those of the pilot's day. Since 2026-11-12 the observation table is hashed
  by its read columns and the prior changed, so the presets have other hashes (CHANGELOG).
