# The PSF on the mask window and the projection in one product: what changes, by how much, how fast

Written on 2026-11-24 on the branch `fastfit` (`2ef14e5`, merged since). Two fit-fix switches, both off by default:

| switch | values | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| `psf_eval` | `image` (default), `window` | the off-axis King PSF computed on the bounding box of each image's mask instead of the whole padded grid (`massfit/psf_window.py`) |
| `projection` | `groups` (default), `matrix` | all node groups of an evaluation projected in one triangular matrix product instead of one matrix-vector product each (`massfit/band_response.py`) |

Neither changes the model. `projection = matrix` returned the same log posterior at every point tested in 85 of 86
fits. `psf_eval = window` changes the model image by the rounding of a single-precision FFT of another length, and
this page measures what that does to a fit. The fits of pass 1 finished by 2026-11-24 were made with both off.

Adopted since. The preset `v1_flamingo_l1m8_pass2_extra` sets `massfit.psf_eval = 'window'` and
`massfit.projection = 'matrix'` (2026-11-25), so the pass-2 views of the list with the added observations are
fitted with both. The last 978 fits of pass 1 were made with both on 2026-11-26, on a second cluster, after a
control sample: 75 finished z = 0.1 mocks refitted with the two switches gave ln M500 (switched minus production)
mean +0.001 +- 0.006, median +0.0005, 38 up and 37 down; two of the 75 moved by more than 0.25. The other fits of
pass 1 (about 4,000, on the home cluster) and the 623 fits of the real observations used the evaluation on the
whole grid.

## Summary

| quantity | measured | section |
|---|---|---|
| log posterior at a fixed point | changes by sd 1e-4 (mocks) and 5e-4 (real) about a constant offset, largest 0.038; one single-precision step of an image term is 0.004 to 0.125 | 2 |
| mass at that change | the reweighted mean of ln M500 moves by at most 6e-5, which is 2.4e-3 of the posterior width | 2 |
| window against the exact convolution | nearer than the image grid in 29 of 40 fits (double-precision reference) | 3 |
| end point of a fit | moves, as it does for any change in the last digit: a start moved by 1e-6 in ln M500 moves the end point as far as the window does (rms 0.013 in ln M500 both) | 4 |
| direction of the move | none found. Window minus image grid from the same start: 22 up, 17 down, mean -0.0012 +- 0.0021. Full fits, new minus cache arm: 25 up, 20 down, mean +0.005 +- 0.004 | 4 |
| posterior widths | unchanged, median ratio 1.00 | 4 |
| reproducibility of a windowed fit | 6 of 6 repeated fits bit-identical; with the image-grid PSF 1 of 5 (FFTW plans chosen by timing) | 5 |
| speed of a fit | windowed fits 3.1 times the unchanged code on the same kind of node (2.3 times the cache alone); others 1.8 times (1.4); weighted by pass-1 run time 2.5 times | 6 |

Everything was measured on fits of pass 1 (FLAMINGO mocks) and of the real sample, with the image cache on. Nothing
here says whether a fit has found its maximum or sampled its posterior; the reruns of unchanged code in section 4
differ by 2 to 4 per cent in M500, and that scatter is larger than any effect of the two switches.

## 1. What is computed

`KingAnglePSF` (`massfit/fit_psf.py`) convolves the cluster image S of every evaluation as

    model = sum_j  C_j (x) [w_j S]   +   sum_j  T_j (*) B[w_j S]

with w_j the hat weight of off-axis node j, C_j the node's core kernel (reach h pixels, cyclic on the padded grid),
B the b x b block sums and T_j the tail transfer on the coarse grid. The transforms run on the whole padded grid:
234 x 234 pixels for a z = 0.1 fit at 12 arcsec, of which a quarter is inside the mask.

The likelihood reads the model inside the mask only (`mbproj2d.fast.calcPoissonLogLikelihoodMasked`). With O the
bounding box of the mask, `WindowedAnglePSF` computes the same sums on O:

- **Core.** An output pixel takes source pixels within h of it, so the output on O needs the source on O grown by h.
  The weight of a node is non-zero on an annulus about the optical axis. Consecutive nodes are grouped, and a group
  is transformed on a buffer that holds the rows and columns where its weights are non-zero, cut to O grown by h.
  For input rows a..b and output rows u1..u2 the offsets output minus input span u1 - b .. u2 - a; a cyclic length
  N >= max(u2 - a, b - u1) + h + 1 keeps every offset larger than h away from the kernel, so the cyclic convolution
  on N equals the linear one at every output row. The same holds for the columns.
- **Tail.** The block sums A..B of a group's input are convolved with the transfer at the offsets Y1 - B .. Y2 - A
  for the output blocks Y1..Y2 of O; N >= (B - A + 1) + (Y2 - Y1 + 1) - 1 keeps them apart.
- **Lengths.** The smallest even 2^a 3^b 5^c at or above these minima. For the 234 px grid the four groups run on
  60, 100, 144 and 180 px (core) and 64, 80, 90 and 128 blocks (tail), two nodes each.
- **Unchanged.** The kernel values (`KingPSF.resample`), the tail transfer (`tail_transfer`), the weights, the
  single-precision products w_j S and the order of the block sums. The spectra of a group are summed and inverted
  once, as `_fourier_sum` does for all nodes.

Two things follow from the construction. No part of the kernel is dropped, so there is no truncation error: the
only difference is rounding. And the model outside O is not computed (it is set to the floor value), so the window
may be used only where the mask is the only reader; the fit switches back to the image-grid PSF after the sampler.

An image whose mask box grown by h reaches the edge of the grid keeps `KingAnglePSF`: there the cyclic wrap of the
grid is part of the model. In the 86 fits of section 2 this is every image of the fits at z >= 0.25 and of most
fits at z = 0.2; all images are windowed in 34 of 36 fits at z <= 0.15 (25 of 25 mocks, 9 of 11 real).

The grouping is chosen by counted work (n^2 log2 n per transform, n^2 per element-wise pass) and the plans are made
with `FFTW_ESTIMATE`, so nothing depends on timing (section 5).

`projection = matrix`: `Radii.project` of mbproj2d multiplies the 1056 x 1056 projection matrix by one emissivity
vector at a time, 61 times an evaluation for a 12-image fit. The matrix is upper triangular, so the emissivities
are stacked as columns and multiplied once with BLAS `dtrmm`, in double precision, then cast to single as before.

## 2. The log posterior at fixed points

86 finished fits (57 mocks, 29 real), 100 points of each fit's stored chain. At each point the log posterior was
computed four ways: as fitted, with the window, with the matrix projection, with both. The per-image terms are
single precision in mbproj2d; they were summed in double precision here, so that the comparison is not limited by
the last addition. A second sum takes the model images and forms the Poisson term in double precision.

**Projection.** Identical at all 100 points in 85 of 86 fits. One real fit differs by at most 1.9e-5. The product
is the same sum in another order, so identity is observed and not guaranteed.

**Window**, the 40 fits in which every image is windowed (26 mocks, 14 real):

| | mocks | real |
|---|---|---|
| sd of the difference over the chain points, median over fits | 1.1e-4 | 4.6e-4 |
| the same, largest fit | 1.0e-3 | 1.1e-2 |
| largest difference at any point | 0.038 | 0.038 |
| in single-precision steps of an image term | up to 1.16 | |
| change of the model flux | 1e-9, largest 3.5e-8 | |
| mean difference positive / negative (fits) | 16 / 10 (p = 0.33) | 8 / 6 (p = 0.79) |
| shift of the mean of ln M500 when the chain is reweighted | at most 1.5e-5 | at most 5.9e-5 |
| the same in units of the posterior sd | at most 2.4e-3 | |
| correlation of the difference with ln M500, median | 0.02 to 0.04 | |

The largest sd belongs to PSZ2 G065.32-64.84 with 1.17 million counts: the term of an image is a number near 1e6
held in single precision, and its last digit is 0.06 to 0.125.

A constant offset of the log posterior changes nothing. What could move a mass is a difference that varies with the
parameters, and the reweighting measures exactly that: weights exp(difference) on the chain points move the mean of
ln M500 by less than a hundredth of a per cent of M500.

In the fits where no image is windowed (z >= 0.25 and most of z = 0.2) `psf_eval = window` changes nothing: the
difference is zero at every point.

## 3. Which of the two is nearer to the exact convolution

The PSF of the fit was recomputed in double precision with numpy's FFT, from the same kernels, weights and
single-precision products (40 fully windowed fits, 40 chain points each).

| | image grid | window |
|---|---|---|
| rms distance of the log posterior from the reference, median over fits | 1.75e-4 | 1.60e-4 |
| largest distance | 7.7e-3 | 6.6e-3 |
| mean offset positive / negative (fits) | 16 / 24 | 20 / 20 |
| shift of the mean of ln M500 against the reference, largest | 7.5e-6 | 5.4e-6 |
| flux against the reference, largest | 4.2e-8 | 3.5e-8 |

The window is nearer to the reference in 29 of 40 fits (Wilcoxon p = 0.001; 20 of 26 mocks, 9 of 14 real). The
transforms are shorter, so fewer rounding errors add up. Neither is biased against the reference at a level that
could be measured.

## 4. Fits

### 4.1 Same start, three optimiser runs

For 39 fully windowed fits (26 mocks, 13 real) stage 2 of the optimiser was run three times from the best sample
of the stored chain: as fitted, with the two switches, and as fitted with the start moved by 1e-6 in ln M500. The
third run has no meaning other than to show what a difference in the sixth digit of one start value does to the
path of a derivative-free optimiser. All end points were then evaluated the same way (image-grid PSF,
double-precision sum).

| end point minus the end point as fitted | window + matrix | start moved by 1e-6 |
|---|---|---|
| ln M500: mean | -0.0012 +- 0.0021 | -0.0015 +- 0.0020 |
| ln M500: rms | 0.0132 | 0.0125 |
| ln M500: median absolute | 0.0056 | 0.0069 |
| ln M500: largest | 0.050 | 0.041 |
| ln M500: up / down | 22 / 17 (p = 0.52) | 18 / 21 (p = 0.75) |
| log posterior: median absolute | 0.10 | 0.12 |
| log posterior: largest | 4.0 | 11.1 |
| log posterior: up / down | 14 / 25 (p = 0.11) | 20 / 19 |
| evaluations, sum, relative to as fitted | 0.957 | 1.088 |

The two columns are the same within their errors (absolute differences in ln M500, paired: Wilcoxon p = 0.49; in
log posterior p = 0.84). The window acts on the optimiser as any last-digit change does: the path branches after
about a hundred evaluations and ends at another point of the same flat maximum, about 1 per cent away in M500.
The evaluation count of section R1 of the speed report (37 per cent more in one run) was one draw of this scatter;
over 39 fits the window needed 4 per cent fewer evaluations and the moved start 9 per cent more.

Job 1902121 (tree `2ef14e5`, both switches, image cache on) repeated 26 mock and 26 real fits that job 1902090 had

The first three rows are the scatter of the fit itself: the same numerics, run again, give another mass in two

0.02 in ln M500 at two sigma for windowed fits. The bound from section 4.1, where the start is shared, is 0.005,
and section 2 puts the direct effect at 6e-5.

## 5. Reproducibility and the FFTW plans

Pass-1 fits are not reproducible to the bit: of 10 mocks rerun with the unchanged code, 4 gave another chain.
The cause is in the image-grid PSF. mbproj2d and the tail of `KingAnglePSF` make their FFTW plans with
`FFTW_MEASURE`, which times candidate algorithms when the fit is set up and keeps the fastest. On a busy node the
timing differs from run to run, another plan sums in another order, and the last digit of the model differs.
Repeats of one PSF evaluation in new processes on the node matched the first in 1 of 10; with the PSF switched off
10 of 10; with the window 9 of 9 and 7 of 7.

`WindowedAnglePSF` plans with `FFTW_ESTIMATE` and chooses its groups by counted work, so the same input gives the
same bits. In job 1902121 the six repeated fits with every image windowed reproduced their chains exactly; of the
five repeated fits without a windowed image one did.

`FFTW_ESTIMATE` costs speed: in separate processes a 234 px transform took 0.335 ms against 0.292 ms with a
measured plan, a 280 px one 0.433 against 0.262. The window is faster than the image grid in spite of that. A plan
measured once and stored as FFTW wisdom, imported by every fit, would give measured speed and fixed bits for both
paths; that is not written.

## 6. Speed

**One evaluation** (login node, one core, cache off): 106.5 ms to 50.4 ms in mocks at z <= 0.15 (median factor
2.09, range 1.70 to 2.20), 1.98 in real fits with every image windowed, 97.7 to 81.0 ms at z = 0.2, and 1.11 to
1.18 where no image is windowed (the projection alone).

**Stage 2 of the optimiser with the cache**, same start (section 4.1, on node cores next to running fits): 2.29
times less time per evaluation, 2.41 in total.

**Whole fits**, run time as recorded by the fit. "plain", "cache" and "new" ran on the same kind of node in two
jobs of 52 and 64 fits; "prod" ran on the pass-1 nodes with 72 fits each.

| time ratio (sum over fits) | every image windowed | no image windowed | all |
|---|---|---|---|
| plain / new | 3.10 (n 6; optimiser 4.66, sampler 1.93) | 1.83 (n 13; 2.50, 1.07) | 2.17 (n 19) |
| cache / new | 2.32 (n 26; 2.65, 2.00) | 1.38 (n 21; 1.54, 1.20) | 1.83 (n 50) |
| prod / new | 4.72 (n 26; 6.45, 3.04) | 2.28 (n 21; 2.90, 1.56) | 3.47 (n 51) |
| plain / cache | 1.30 (n 6) | 1.40 (n 13) | 1.36 (n 19) |
| new / rep | 1.00 (n 6) | 1.04 (n 5) | 1.03 (n 11) |

The 51 fits took 287.6 core hours in production, 147.0 with the cache and 81.6 with the cache and both switches.

The 3,976 finished pass-1 fits spent 63 per cent of their 21,763 core hours at z = 0.1, 23 per cent at z = 0.2 and
14 per cent above. With the factors of the first row (windowed at z = 0.1, not windowed above) the campaign would
take 1 / 2.5 of its time; with the third row 1 / 3.4. The first is the like-for-like number. The fits still to run
on 2026-11-24 were mostly the long ones at z = 0.1, which moves the figure up a little.

Two cautions. Between 03:30 and 04:00 of the job other processes of this test held more cores than were free
(load 75 to 77 on 72 cores), so fits running then lost a few per cent. And the non-windowed fits gained more on
the node (1.38 over the cache) than one evaluation gains on the login node (1.11 to 1.18): the single product
moves about 540 MB less memory per evaluation, which matters when 72 fits share the memory bus, but this was not
measured separately.

## 7. Limits

- The window needs the mask box grown by the kernel reach inside the grid. At z = 0.2 the masks reach the edge in
  most mocks (7 of 9 here), and those fits take 23 per cent of the campaign time. Padding the buffer instead
  of refusing would extend the window to them; the cyclic wrap of the image grid would then be absent from the
  model, which is a change of the model at the edge and not of the rounding, and needs its own test.
- The fit does not record how many images were windowed; it prints the count. It should go into `result.json`.
- A windowed PSF fills the mask box only. The fit puts the image-grid PSF back after the sampler; code that reads
  model images during the fit outside the mask would get the floor value.
- Mixed fits (some images windowed) and fits without a windowed image keep `FFTW_MEASURE` plans for the rest and
  are not reproducible to the bit.
- About 4,000 fits of pass 1 were made with both switches off and the last 978 with both on (2026-11-26). A sample
  fitted partly with and partly without them is one method to within the numbers above; the switch is recorded per
  fit (`fitfix` in `result.json`).
- Not tested: more than 12 images, pixel sizes other than 8 to 24 arcsec, the fast-fit presets.

## 8. How to reproduce

Unit tests: `tests/unit/test_massfit_psf_window.py` (window against image grid inside the mask for three
geometries, bit identity of two windows, edge masks, switch defaults) and `tests/unit/test_massfit_band_response.py`
(matrix against group projection).

A fit with the switches:

    python -m tngxmm.massfit.fit_hydrostatic ... --fitfix psf_eval=window,projection=matrix

The measurements of this page were made with scripts kept outside the package, in `psfwin/` of the campaign's
development directory: `surface.py` and `surface_summary.py` (section 2), `refcheck.py` and `ref_summary.py`
(section 3), `opt_pairs_node.py` and `opt_summary.py` (4.1), `pairs.py` and `pairs_split.py` with `nwin.py`
(4.2 and 6), `repro.py` and `fftplan.py` (5), `campaign.py` (6). The fits are those of jobs 1902090 (plain, cache)
and 1902121 (new, rep), plan `FIT_PLAN_fastfit_test2.txt`.
