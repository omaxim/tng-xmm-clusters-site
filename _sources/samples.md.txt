# Samples and selection

Which simulated halos and which real observations enter, how they are paired, what each target list is used for, and
the selection effects that are not modelled. Figure: {doc}`figures` (samples).

## The simulated sample

TNG-Cluster (Nelson et al. 2024, Sect. 2.2) re-simulates 352 halos of a 1 Gpc dark-matter-only parent box. They were
chosen on their **z = 0** mass alone: every halo above M200c = 1e15 Msun in the volume, plus halos drawn at random in
0.1 dex bins so that, together with TNG300, the distribution in halo mass is flat from 10^14.3 to 10^15 Msun. The
pipeline takes the primary zoom target of each re-simulation at the nine full snapshots (z = 0 to 1.5) wherever its
M500c is at least 1e14 Msun: 2,463 halo-snapshots (350 at z = 0, 292 at z = 0.5, 67 at z = 1.5).

Consequences:

* At z > 0 the sample is the main progenitors of massive z = 0 clusters, not a mass-complete sample at that epoch:
  halos that are massive at z = 1 but do not grow into the z = 0 selection are missing, and the progenitors are
  biased towards early-forming, fast-growing systems. Formation history and dynamical state affect the hydrostatic
  bias, so a bias measured on these halos at z > 0 is that of the progenitor population.
* The mass function is not cosmological: by construction it is flat in log mass over 10^14.3-10^15 Msun at z = 0.
  No weighting to a cosmological mass function is applied. TNG300 (a periodic box, every FoF central above the mass
  cut) is the volume-limited comparison: 742 halo-snapshots in the maximal list.
* Each halo-snapshot is projected along the three simulation axes (three views); the views of one halo are not
  independent. Folds and bootstrap resamples that group them take every view of a halo, at every snapshot, together
  (release 1.2, `donors.halo_groups`): the 3,258 halo-snapshots of the maximal and box lists are 535 halos (352
  TNG-Cluster zoom targets, 171 TNG300, 11 TNG100 and 1 TNG50 z = 0 objects).

What the selection does to the hydrostatic bias is measured against TNG300, which runs the same physics on every
halo above the mass cut (`internal/ledger_tests/progenitor_bias.py`, v1.0.1 trees). Within each snapshot the TNG300
halo-snapshots are reweighted to the M500c distribution of the TNG-Cluster ones in the mass range both cover (0.1 dex
bins), and the medians of ln(M_HSE / M500c) at R500c are compared:

| z_snap | TNG-Cluster / TNG300 halo-snapshots | TNG-Cluster minus TNG300 [ln] | 95 per cent interval |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 203 / 154 | -0.015 | -0.074, +0.031 |
| 0.1 | 229 / 137 | -0.009 | -0.049, +0.037 |
| 0.2 | 211 / 109 | -0.066 | -0.123, -0.018 |
| 0.3 | 250 / 105 | -0.052 | -0.086, +0.025 |
| 0.4 | 227 / 79 | -0.020 | -0.059, +0.041 |
| 0.5 | 170 / 66 | -0.037 | -0.119, +0.046 |
| 0.7 | 194 / 37 | -0.066 | -0.143, +0.013 |
| 1.0 | 80 / 21 | -0.064 | -0.135, +0.018 |
| all, matched within each snapshot | 1,564 / 708 | -0.032 | -0.050, -0.011 |

At the same mass and redshift the TNG-Cluster halos have an intrinsic hydrostatic mass about 3 per cent lower (6-7
per cent at z = 0.2 and above 0.7, 1-2 per cent at z <= 0.1), consistent with progenitors of massive clusters growing
faster than the average halo of their mass. A bias measured on TNG-Cluster above z = 0 therefore
overstates the population bias by this amount; the TNG300 halos are the population sample (they reach only about
4e14 Msun above z = 0.5). Overall medians: 0.883 (TNG-Cluster) and 0.914 (TNG300), {doc}`figures` (hse_bias_truth).

## The real sample

638 archival XMM-Newton observations of 611 clusters, selected by their catalogue: Planck PSZ2 (414 observations),
ACT DR6 (207), CCCP (10) and Weighing the Giants (7); z = 0.045 to 1.48, median 0.28; catalogue M500 from 1.6e14 to
2.1e15 Msun, median 4.9e14 Msun ({doc}`data`, `obs.observations`). Every one of them is an observation that someone
proposed and that was carried out, so the sample inherits the selection of the XMM archive (bright, nearby, interesting
or follow-up targets) on top of the SZ or lensing selection. This selection is not modelled.

These 638 are the bank (`in_bank` True in `src/tngxmm/obs/catalogue/observations.csv`). On 2026-11-25 an archive
search added 170 observations to the table (`in_bank` False, `sample` and `mass_class` = `extra`; inputs under
`src/tngxmm/obs/catalogue/inputs_extra/`, module `obs/extra_observations.py`), which now has 808 rows. Every number
of this page is for the bank; the target lists below pair with the bank only. Of the bank, 623 observations have a
hydrostatic fit; the 170 added ones are being fitted with the same settings.

### How the added observations were chosen

The archive search of 2026-11-20 matched clusters of MCXC-II, eRASS1, ACT DR6, PSZ2 and the SPT catalogues that the
bank does not hold with public XMM-Newton observations and left 194 candidates. Each was then graded in three
steps (`obs/candidate_quality.py`, `tngxmm obs grade-candidates`):

1. Measurements on the pipeline images, 0.5-2 keV summed over the cameras, with the same function as for the bank:
   the exposure at the catalogue position, the coverage of R500, a background rate beyond 1.3 R500, the counts
   inside 0.5 R500, the peak of the smoothed image, and the 4.5-12 keV rate beyond 1.3 R500 per camera.
2. Flags from these numbers. The thresholds are screening values set for this search; none is fitted to a result:

   | flag | set when |
   |---|---|
   | `FAIL:no_images` | the archive products hold no per-camera image with its exposure map |
   | `FAIL:clean_exposure<5ks` | the longest camera exposure after the pipeline's flare screening is below 5 ks |
   | `short_exposure` | that exposure is between 5 and 10 ks |
   | `FAIL:centre_off_detector` | the median exposure within 0.5 arcmin of the position is below 0.5 of the maximum of the exposure map |
   | `cameras:<present>` | fewer than three cameras have images |
   | `r500_cover<0.9` | less than 0.9 of the disc of radius theta500 has an exposure above 0.2 of the maximum |
   | `no_offsource_region` | fewer than 20 background blocks of 8 x 8 pixels lie beyond 1.3 theta500, so there is no background rate |
   | `FAIL?:not_detected` | (counts - background) / sqrt(counts) inside 0.5 theta500 is below 5 |
   | `point_like_core` | more than 0.6 of the net counts of 0.5 theta500 lie within 20 arcsec of the peak |
   | `peak_far_from_position` | the peak is further from the catalogue position than max(1 arcmin, 0.3 theta500) |
   | `high_particle_bg` | the 4.5-12 keV rate of a camera beyond 1.3 theta500 is above the 95th percentile of the bank's rates for that camera (0.0500 pn, 0.0081 MOS1, 0.0068 MOS2 counts/s/arcmin2) |
   | `window_mode` | the pn sub-mode is a window mode other than full window, or a MOS sub-mode is a partial window |

3. A grade by eye. Contact sheets of all 194 candidates (the smoothed image with circles at 0.5 and 1 theta500 and
   the flags) were inspected, and each candidate got `pass`, `reject` or `hold` in the column `grade` and, where the
   case was not plain, a sentence in `eye_note`. This step is manual. Its criteria are not written down beyond those
   notes, and the grade is not a function of the flags: 170 passed, 13 were rejected, 11 held; two candidates with a
   clean exposure of 3.6 and 4.0 ks passed (signal-to-noise 51 and 28), and nine without a `FAIL` flag were
   rejected because the emission is a point source or a foreground galaxy.

`inputs_extra/candidates_graded.csv` holds all 194 rows with the measurements, the flags (without the `FAIL`
prefixes), `grade` and `eye_note`. The 170 rows with `grade` = `pass` are the added observations; `hold` rows are
not used. The selection is therefore reproducible from that file, and the flags from the images, but the grade
itself only by looking again.

A donor-matched comparison removes part of it: each mock is observed under the conditions of a real observation and
compared with that observation, so exposure, background, field and redshift are matched by construction. It does not
remove a selection on cluster properties (luminosity, morphology, cool core) that correlates with mass at fixed
catalogue mass, because the mocks are paired by mass and redshift only. X-ray selection favours cool cores at fixed
mass (Rossetti et al. 2017: 59 per cent cool cores in MACS against 29 per cent in a Planck SZ sample), and an archive of
pointed observations can carry part of it. The paper states this as a limitation and bounds it with a rule written
before the comparison: at z < 0.35 the mock-against-real comparison is also given separately for cool-core and
non-cool-core clusters, classified on both sides by the same photometry with c = F(< 40 kpc) / F(< 400 kpc) and the
threshold 0.075 of Santos et al. (2008) as used by Rossetti et al., together with the cool-core fractions of the real
pool and of the paired mocks. Above z = 0.35 XMM-Newton does not resolve the 40 kpc core (Rossetti et al. leave those
clusters out), so c is given there as a same-photometry label, not as a class. Ledger entry: `samples.archive_selection`.

## Pairing: the matching mass

A halo-snapshot is paired with a reference observation when their masses and redshifts are close (median
|delta log M| = 0.08 dex, 90th percentile 0.19). The mass of the real side is the "matching mass": the catalogue
M500 divided by (1 - b) = 0.78 for PSZ2 clusters (the CCCP baseline of Planck Collaboration 2016, Planck 2015
results XXIV, Table 2; 1,887 of the 2,840 replica views), and the
catalogue M500c for ACT DR6 (Sect. 2.5 of the ACT DR6 cluster catalogue; these masses are already calibrated to
weak lensing, which corresponds to a factor of about 0.65 on the SZ scaling relation according to the ledger entry)
and for the lensing catalogues. Ledger entry:
`donors.matching_mass`. The two SZ halves therefore differ by a factor of about 1.2 in mass scale.

This catalogue matching mass belongs to the replica, maximal and box lists. Since the decision of 2026-10-27 the
production pairing matches on the fitted hydrostatic mass on both sides, with no catalogue mass and no bias factor.
Pass 1 gives every FLAMINGO L1_m8 halo-snapshot a fitted mass from one view; pass 2 pairs each of its six
icosahedral views with a different observation of the window in redshift and fitted mass (`tngxmm donors
pair-projections`, `donors/pair_projections.py`; under `--assignment least-used` the observations of the window
that serve the fewest views so far). The pass-2 list that includes the 170 added observations is not built yet: it
waits for their fits.

What depends on this assumed bias: which halo is paired with which observation, and through that every comparison at
fixed observation (realism, count matching, mock against real fits). What does not: the bias estimands of a mock
(M_X, M_HSE and M_true of the same halo, {doc}`definitions`). The sensitivity test S7 redrew the replica list with
(1 - b) = 0.84 and 0.90 ({doc}`assumptions/SENSITIVITY_TESTS`, R12): gate P fails at every value (mass D 0.241, 0.229,
0.207), the median halo mass of the views moves by -0.008 and -0.018 dex, and almost every individual pairing
changes. A population result therefore depends weakly on the assumed bias, a result about "the mock of this real
cluster" strongly.

## Gate P: the replica list fails, the matched draw passes

Gate P compares the views of a list with the reference observations (Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance D against a
bootstrap critical value; `campaigns/v4/gate_p.md`). For the replica list:

* redshift, live times, N_H, theta500, filters and modes pass;
* the matching mass fails: D = 0.241 against a critical 0.061. The heaviest real clusters have too few TNG-Cluster
  halos of their mass at their redshift, so the mocks are lighter on average;
* coverage fails: 593 of the 638 observations (93 per cent) have views, below the 95 per cent rule. 14 ACT clusters
  at z = 1.16-1.34 have no snapshot within the +-0.15 window (snapshots 50 and 40 are 0.5 apart), and 432 views use
  the nearest snapshot outside the window.

No variant of K or of the window passes the mass test on the nine full snapshots. Decision of release 1.1.3: every
population statistic uses the **matched draw** (`campaigns/v4_max/sampler_recommended_*`: K' = 5 views per
observation drawn from the maximal list, each view at most 12 times, |z_obs - z_snap| <= 0.25, TNG-Cluster and
TNG300; 3,111 draws, 2,921 of TNG-Cluster and 190 of TNG300), and the replica list keeps its role in the realism
pairing, where each mock is compared with its own donor. Recomputed with the current code from the committed inputs,
the matched draw passes gate P strictly: matching mass D = 0.026 (critical 0.067), redshift 0.036 (0.069), live times,
N_H, theta500, filters and modes all within their critical values, coverage 0.978.

How much the failure of the replica list matters for the bias: the intrinsic M_HSE(< R500c) / M500c has a median of
0.877 on the replica views and 0.885 on the matched draw (v1.0.1 trees), a difference of under 1 per cent.

## Snapshot redshift and observed redshift

A view is the gas of z_snap observed at z_obs ({doc}`definitions`). Replica list: |z_obs - z_snap| has a median of
0.033 and a 90th percentile of 0.088. The estimands are defined at 500 rho_c(z_obs) for the true, the hydrostatic and
the X-ray mass alike, so the comparison of the three is consistent; what changes is the halo the comparison is
about. On the 9,774 views of the maximal and box lists as generated (release 1.0.1 trees), M500c at 500 rho_c(z_obs)
differs from M500c at 500 rho_c(z_snap) of the same particles by a median of +0.001 in ln M (16-84 per cent -0.036 to
+0.042, 5-95 per cent -0.062 to +0.073), negative when z_obs > z_snap. The gas itself (its temperature, structure and
dynamical state) stays that of z_snap. Its effect is measured on the truth of the production tree. Within one
halo-snapshot the views differ only in z_obs, so a line through their ln(M500c,HSE / M500c) against z_obs - z_snap
gives the change of the definition at fixed gas (+0.52 per unit z in the median) and an intercept, the bias with the
definition of z_snap. Between neighbouring snapshots of one TNG-Cluster halo the intercept, less the definitional
change, moves by -0.61 per unit redshift in the median (bootstrap 95 per cent -0.66 to -0.53; 2,108 snapshot pairs of
350 zoom targets): that is the gas-only slope k. The term k (z_obs - z_snap) then has a mean of -0.0012 over 7,374
views (-0.0033 when halo-snapshots with too few views for a line enter with their median), because the views are
displaced in both directions (mean z_obs - z_snap +0.001). In the three redshift bins z_snap < 0.25, 0.25-0.6 and
above 0.6 the mean displacement is +0.012, -0.015 and +0.008, so a bin moves by at most 0.01. If every view were
displaced the same way the shift would be 0.033 (|k| times the median |dz| of 0.054). Single halos scatter widely
(16-84 per cent of k -3.2 to +2.1, mergers between snapshots), so |k dz| of one view (median 0.044) bounds the added
scatter from above. The paper states the term as measured: -0.001 to -0.003 in ln for the whole population, and for
every subsample it reports the mean k dz of that subsample; a subsample where it exceeds 0.03 carries it as an arm
under the pre-registered arm rule (delta = 0.03). MACSIS has the same sign with a smaller size (the T-M normalisation
15 per cent lower at z = 1 than at z = 0; Barnes et al. 2017), a different quantity in a different simulation. The
direct comparison with the 60-snapshot `v1_common` views (|dz| median 0.006) is not part of the first paper. Script
`internal/ledger_tests/c4_gas_state_zobs.py`; ledger entry `method.snapshot_to_zobs`.

## Other simulations

FLAMINGO, SIMBA and EAGLE are periodic boxes, so their halo samples are volume-limited at every snapshot; their
presets use the snapshots nearest the nine TNG redshifts. The FLAMINGO L1_m8 sample was drawn before any streaming
to fill the (mass, redshift) plane of the 638 real observations: centrals from SOAP-HBT, every halo above 6e14
Msun, and below it random halos in cells of 0.1 dex in true M500c from 1.2e14 Msun per snapshot, weighted by the
density of real observations, at least 8 per cell that real observations reach: 5,000 halo-snapshots of 2,143
halos, with at least six candidates for 611 of the 638 observations. The other 27 are the most massive clusters and
those above z = 1.25, of which the 1 Gpc box holds too few. The production halo lists of SIMBA and EAGLE are not drawn yet. Rule and
tables: [DECISIONS, FLAMINGO L1_m8](dev/DECISIONS.md#flamingo-l1_m8-the-production-flamingo-source-streamed-sample-rule-stated-before-the-streaming).
