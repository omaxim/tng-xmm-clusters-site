# Instrument background of the mocks

Each mock view carries the background of the real observation whose conditions it is observed under (the
conditions donor).  This page lists the components, where each level comes from, and the factors that were removed
at the integration of 2026-10-30 because they were measured on replays or fitted on donors rather than taken from
calibration data.

## Components

| component | level | spatial form | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| quiescent particle background (QPB), every camera | the donor's unexposed corners per PPS band, relative to the corners of the CCF filter-wheel-closed (FWC) event lists; a camera without a usable corner level takes the FWC level | FWC event density per band (`instrument.qpb_spatial = 'fwc'`) | XMM-ESAS mosback / pnback (Snowden et al. 2008; Kuntz & Snowden 2008); table `donor_qpb_corners.csv` |
| residual soft protons, MOS | IN/OUT excess at 10-12 keV over the corner QPB and the CXB | the CCF flare maps of the filter and band, smoothed 1' (`instrument.soft_proton_map = 'flare'`) | De Luca & Molendi (2004); Kuntz & Snowden (2008); table `donor_mos_background.csv` |
| residual soft protons, pn | the same IN/OUT excess, a lower bound without validation (below) | as the MOS | De Luca & Molendi (2004); Marelli et al. (2021); ledger `instrument.pn_soft_protons_inout` |
| sky: local hot bubble, Galactic halo | the donor field's levels from its RASS spectrum in the 1-2 deg annulus, at the published median temperatures (0.111, 0.191 keV) | vignetted | Snowden et al. (2008); Yeung et al. (2024); Henley & Shelton (2013); table `donor_sky_rass.csv`; ledger `instrument.sky_background` |
| sky: unresolved CXB | the Moretti et al. (2003) 0.5-2 keV total less the AGN drawn (23 per cent), photon index 1.41, absorbed by the donor's total Galactic column | vignetted | Moretti et al. (2003); De Luca & Molendi (2004) |
| field AGN | Moretti et al. (2003) soft-band counts | the SAS elliptical PSF | ledger `instrument.point_sources` |
| pn out-of-time events | the Users Handbook fraction of the readout mode; energies with the CTI correction of the assigned row | uniform along the column of the event | Users Handbook Sect. 3.3.10; SAS epevents; CCF EPN_CTI (below) |

The corner levels are those of the instrument stage and of the fit's ESAS background model
(`massfit.qpb_model = 'esas-fwc-corners'`, the presets' fit background): the corner count rate of each MOS CCD or pn quadrant,
within the flare intervals of the PPS images, over the corner rate of the FWC lists on the same CCDs and in the same
band.  The pn corner counts have the out-of-time events of the in-field X-rays subtracted column by column.  The
in-field over corner ratio of the QPB is a detector property (Leccardi & Molendi 2008, App. B), so the FWC spectrum
scaled band by band with the corners is the in-field QPB of the observation.  On the field of 471 real observations
beyond 2 R500 the corner-scaled FWC template lies below the data, as a background component must: median
template / data 0.90 for the pn in 4.5-12 keV, 0.88 and 0.92 for MOS1 and MOS2 (job 1890062; DECISIONS, "The pn
corner level: the cause of the excess").

## The pn Cu-line gap

The PPS pn images in 4.5-12 keV leave out PI 7.8-8.2 keV, the Cu K-alpha line of the pn particle background
(selection in the XPROC0 history of the images; Mateos et al. 2009, Sect. 2, for the 2XMM processing).  Since the
integration every preset applies the same selection to the mock pn images and to the fit's pn band
(`instrument.pn_hard_band = 'pps'`, `massfit.pn_hard_band = 'pps'`).  The gap holds 28 per cent of the FWC pn rate
in 4.5-12 keV and 1.4-3.6 per cent of the cluster counts.  Before it, the v1 mocks' pn hard-band field was 31 per
cent brighter than the real one, about what the Cu line adds to a contiguous band.

## Residual soft protons of the pn

The donor table `donor_mos_background.csv` holds the pn as well as the MOS (`donor_tables.mos_background.cameras`).
The pn excess is measured exactly as the MOS one: the in-field 10-12 keV rate beyond max(1.5 R500, 3') of the
cluster and within 12' of the pointing, within the PPS flare intervals, minus the corner-scaled FWC in-field rate
and the CXB (De Luca & Molendi 2004).  The 10-12 keV band lies above the Cu-line gap of the pn hard band, so the pn
fluorescence lines do not enter it.  The stage turns the excess into a soft-proton rate with the quiescent
soft-proton spectrum of Leccardi & Molendi (2008) and spreads it flat over the field of view, as for the MOS.

The pn value is a lower bound and has no validation:

* the pn corners are not shielded from the sky.  Marelli et al. (2021, Sect. 2) find focused soft protons and photons
  over the whole pn out-of-field area, most near the edge of the field.  The corner scale therefore overestimates the
  pn particle level, the particle rate subtracted from the in-field rate is too high, and the excess too low, the
  more so the stronger the soft protons;
* the method papers (De Luca & Molendi 2004; Leccardi & Molendi 2008) use MOS data only, no source calibrates a pn
  IN/OUT excess against a soft-proton level, and no measured pn quiescent soft-proton spectrum exists, so the pn
  takes the MOS spectrum.

The data package has held the table with the pn columns since 2026-11-03 (provenance in
`donor_mos_background.provenance.json`).  A table measured before the pn was added has no pn columns, and the stage
refuses it when the pn is simulated; it used to leave the pn without soft protons.  The table with the pn columns
comes from

    kedro run --pipeline donor_tables --nodes donor_tables.build_fwc_reference,donor_tables.measure_mos_background,donor_tables.build_mos_background

(`docs/dev/KEDRO.md`; shardable with `--params donor_tables.mos_background.shard=k/n`), after which
`donor_mos_background.csv` and its line of `SHA256SUMS` in the data package are replaced.  Every donor is measured
again, because the camera list enters the signature of the stored results.

One replay measured the effect (job 1891399, 199 TNG views with and without the pn rate, 638 donors measured, 511
with a finite pn rate).  Median log10 of the pn background beyond 8' over the donor's:

| pn band | without pn soft protons | with |
|---|---|---|
| 0.2-0.5 keV | +0.032 | +0.074 |
| 0.5-1 keV | -0.021 | +0.025 |
| 1-2 keV | -0.079 | +0.011 |
| 2-4.5 keV | -0.093 | +0.029 |
| 4.5-12 keV | -0.095 | +0.003 |

The MOS are unchanged.  The soft-band background ratio pn / (MOS1 + MOS2) rises from 1.05 to 1.18, the real data
give 1.32; the background hardness rises from 1.63 to 1.72 against 1.68 in the real data.  A pn level still below
the real one fits a lower bound; the hardness says the MOS spectrum is somewhat too hard for the pn.  This is one replay, not a validation of the pn level.
The replay used the table measured on 2026-10-30; the table was measured again at the final centres on 2026-11-03
(next section).

## Donor tables measured about the cluster

Six donor tables depend on where the real cluster is.  The core offsets (`donor_core_offsets.csv`) place the mock
cluster on the detector; the anomalous-CCD table (`donor_soft_ccd.csv`) and the in-field background table
(`donor_background.csv`) are measured beyond 1.5 R500 of the cluster and the IN/OUT soft-proton rates
(`donor_mos_background.csv`) beyond max(1.5 R500, 3') of it; the corner QPB table (`donor_qpb_corners.csv`) is
measured for the donors of the same catalogue; the condition table (`donor_conditions.csv`) copies the core offsets
and the background levels, dimensions of the condition space.  All take the centre of the real fits, the guarded
centre of `obs_centres_guarded_v4.csv`.  They are written with a provenance sidecar (`<table>.provenance.json`: the
input files with their sha256, relative to the data package or the repository, the commit, `git_dirty`, and for the
measured tables the measurement version and, for the two corner tables, the rule of the FWC reference), into the
data package only from a committed, clean tree (`core.provenance.refuse_dirty_data_write`; `--allow-dirty` or
`allow_dirty: true` in a catalog entry for development), and the instrument stage, the conditions donor and the fits
read the data-package copy only when the sidecar says `git_dirty` false, records the current measurement version and
FWC rule, and names the current centre and seed tables (the core offsets: the centre table and `observations.csv`;
the two corner tables: also the anomalous-CCD table; the condition table: also the current core-offset and
background tables; `instrument.donor_tables.check_table`, `donors.build_core_offsets.check_core_offsets_table`).  The
inputs must be current themselves: an input of the data package whose own sidecar records `git_dirty` true is
refused, and so is the centre table when it was not copied from the current seed table or that seed table is not
current for the production preset (`photometry.build_obs_centres_guarded.check_centres_table`, which calls
`photometry.build_obs_seed_lx.check_seed_table`).  The real fit reads the corner table through the same check.

The other files a view is made from are checked too (`sim.data_inputs`): the total column, the RASS sky, the active
CCDs and the CCD maps, the source thresholds (also their measurement version and columns) and the catalogue positions
(also their redshift and position rules and columns) by their sidecars, as above, and their readers refuse a stale
package copy; the APEC tables, the ECF table and the reddening table of the package, which come from outside it
(AtomDB, the frozen release-3 table, IRSA), and the AtomDB files the photon spectra are drawn from (the APEC
continuum and lines of the preset's version, the ionisation balance and abundance files pyatomdb opens) by their
sha256, pinned in `sim/data_manifest.csv`, which is part of the spec hash.  Each view, and each closure cell, records
the sha256 of every one of them (`data_inputs` of `obs_meta.json` or `truth.json`), and a stored view or cell is
reused only when that record is the current one.  The campaign plan makes these
checks before any view, so a table measured under an older version stops the plan, as the real fit stops at a corner
row without its FWC epoch.

The tables are built in this order, from the merged, committed tip:

1. the catalogue positions, `python -m tngxmm.photometry.catalogue_positions --psz2 ... --dr6 ... --counterparts
   $TNGXMM_SITE_REFS/psz2_counterparts/psz2_counterpart_redshifts.csv` (the counterpart table from
   `tools/psz2_counterpart_redshifts.py`);
2. the active CCDs with the CCD maps, `tngxmm donors active-ccds`, and the source thresholds,
   `tngxmm massfit build-source-thresholds --pps-cache ...` (their sha256 enters the seed hash);
3. the real seed table, `tngxmm photometry obs-seed-lx --preset v1`;
4. the guarded centres, `tngxmm photometry obs-centres-guarded`;
5. the core offsets, `kedro run --pipeline donors --nodes donors.build_core_offsets`;
6. the donor tables, first the PPS event lists and flare series the corner and IN/OUT measurements read
   (`fetch_pps_archive`: only what the cache lacks; its donor list comes from the guarded centres), then the tables
   (shard `measure_mos_background` and `build_qpb_corners` with `--params donor_tables.<node's parameters>.shard=k/n`,
   then run them unsharded):

       kedro run --pipeline donor_tables --nodes donor_tables.fetch_pps_archive,donor_tables.build_donor_nh_total,donor_tables.build_donor_background,donor_tables.build_donor_soft_ccd,donor_tables.build_fwc_reference,donor_tables.measure_mos_background,donor_tables.build_mos_background,donor_tables.build_qpb_corners,donor_tables.build_donor_conditions

7. the RASS sky after the total column, `python -m tngxmm.donor_tables.rass_sky <work> --out
   $TNGXMM_DATA/tables/donor_sky_rass.csv`;
8. `realism.frozen.conditions_table_sha256` registered again;
9. every mock view and every closure cell generated again.

### Donors without a soft-proton rate

The IN/OUT rate is missing where 1.5 R500 of a nearby cluster covers the 12' circle about the pointing (112 of the 588
donors of the conditions pool, all three cameras) or where the pn has no usable corner level (4 more: 0506210101,
0780291001, 0502790101, 0810231001, pn only).  These observations had soft protons like any other: 98.7 to 99.2 per
cent of the measured rates are positive.  A rate of 0, which the stage used to give them, is therefore the lowest
possible value rather than a measurement, and it falls on the nearby clusters: 90 per cent of the pool donors at
z < 0.1 have no rate in some camera, 6 per cent at 0.1-0.2, 1 per cent at z >= 0.2.

The presets (`instrument.sp_rate_unmeasured = 'nearest-in-fold'`) give such a camera the measured rate of the nearest
donor of the conditions pool in the same donor fold that has one (condition-space distance from the donor, ties by
obsid).  The view records it: `SPRATE` is the lent rate, `SPRFLAG` says `substitute`, `SPSUBST` names the lending
observation, and `soft_protons` of `obs_meta.json` and of the conditions record holds the rate, its source and the
distance of each camera.  The alternative `'refuse'` stops such a view, and the plan-time check of the donor tables
lists the donors it would stop.  No rate is set to 0 for want of a measurement.  What the rule gives (tables of
2026-11-03):

* 116 pool donors take a substitute (112 in all three cameras, 4 for the pn); the lending donor is their nearest
  measured neighbour in the median (the fourth at the 90th percentile), at a condition-space distance of 2.63 (10-90th
  percentile 1.72-3.93, at most 5.68), nearer than their own fifth condition neighbour (3.34);
* the lent rates have the median of the measured ones (MOS1 7.05e-4 against 6.54e-4, MOS2 6.86e-4 against 6.49e-4,
  pn 1.81e-3 against 1.82e-3 counts/s/arcmin2 in 0.5-12 keV), as expected of a property of the observation (its
  orbit phase and flare screening) rather than of the cluster;
* 458 of the 2835 replica views (16 per cent) and about 1850 of the 9584 views of the maximal list (19 per cent) had a
  rate of 0 in some camera before and carry a lent rate now.

Leaving such donors out of the conditions pool instead would keep every view to one observation, but the pool would
shrink from 588 to 472 donors, the conditions donor would change for 44 per cent of the replica views and 49 per cent
of the maximal list (mostly through the robust spreads of the condition space, which the pool sets), and the clusters
that fill the field would be observed under smaller ones' conditions: for physics donors with theta500 above 8' (median
10.6') the median theta500 of the conditions donor would fall from 8.7' to 5.9'.

## Energies of the pn out-of-time events

An out-of-time event is registered while its CCD is read out.  Its charge travels the rows between where the photon
landed and the readout node, but `epevents` gives it the RAWY of the readout phase and corrects it for the charge
transfer inefficiency of that row: the Users Handbook (Sect. 3.3.10) says out-of-time events "get a wrong RAWY value
assigned and thus finally a wrong CTI correction", and `epevents withoutoftime=yes` builds the official out-of-time
list the same way ("a new RAWY is simulated by randomly shifting the pattern along the RAWY axis and performing the
gain and CTI correction afterwards").  The stage does what `epevents` does.  With c(A, y) the ratio of corrected to
raw amplitude at row y for raw amplitude A, an event of energy E that landed at row y_t and is registered at y_r gets

    E_OoT = E c(A, y_r) / c(A, y_t),    A = E / c(A, y_t).

c is not ours: `tools/pn_cti/build_pn_cti_probe.sh` links a short program against the SAS 22.1.0 libraries and runs
isolated events through the two RAWY-dependent CAL routines that `epevents` calls (`CAL_pnCtiCorrect`,
`CAL_pnAdditionalGainCtiCorrect`, with the task's defaults: CTI accuracy level 2, long-term CTI with its soft-energy
and RAWY terms) on the CCF in use (EPN_CTI_0058), for the four pn imaging modes, every CCD and the epochs
2000-04-01 and 1 January 2001 to 2027.  The CAL state carries the CCD temperature, on which the CTI model depends
(at 1 keV k of CCD 4 in 2002 is 8.0e-4 per row at 183 K, 7.3e-4 at 193 K and 3.1e-4 at 213 K): the probe sets it to
183.2 K, the pn focal-plane temperature of the housekeeping parameter F1128 in the PPS event lists (-89.95 C at every
epoch read), as `epevents` does; Dennerl et al. (2002) give -90 C as the operating temperature in orbit.  Along a
column ln c is a straight line in RAWY to 1 per cent at most, so the table
(`tngxmm/instrument/calibration/pn_cti_slopes.csv`, module `tngxmm.instrument.pn_oot_energy`) stores its slope
k and intercept per mode, epoch, CCD and amplitude, and

    E_OoT = E exp(k(A) (y_r - y_t)).

The temperature correction of `epevents` multiplies all rows of a CCD alike and the gain of a readout channel does
not depend on RAWY, so neither enters the ratio.  Values (Full Frame, 2010, mean of the twelve CCDs): k = 1.09e-3
per row at 0.5 keV, 9.2e-4 at 1 keV, 7.6e-4 at 2 keV, 5.8e-4 at 5 keV, and k grows with time (8.7e-4 at 1 keV in
2001, 1.01e-3 in 2025).  An event of 0.75 keV that lands next to the row boundary (RAWY 190) and is registered at
RAWY 13 loses 17 per cent of its energy, where the removed 1.4e-3 per row took 22 per cent.  The table is checked
against the pipeline itself: in the PPS event lists the slope in RAWY of ln(PI / 5 PHA) of single-pixel events, the
correction `epevents` applied, is 0.990 times the table's k (16-84 per cent 0.974-1.008, 21 observations of
2000-2025; `tools/pn_cti/pps_cti_slope_check.py` and its unit test).  The table of 2026-10-30, evaluated without
the CCD temperature, had k 1/1.24 of the PPS slope.  The fit's out-of-time template (`massfit.pn_oot_energy`) moves
its counts between the bands with the same k at each band's centre, averaged over the CCDs, at the image's `SUBMODE`
and `DATE-OBS`.  Within a CCD the column mean of k is used (the 16-84 per cent range over the columns of CCD 4 is
about +-8 per cent).  Left out: the precursor dependence of the CTI correction (with an event ahead in its frame
and column the CAL lowers the correction by 2-4 per cent, but the source columns of real streaks carry 0.005 events
per column and frame, so it moves the streak hardness by less than 0.003) and the pixel-by-pixel correction of split
events.

The streak hardness follows its column.  The statistic of the e2e test, HR(1-2 keV / 0.5-1 keV) of the streak far
from the source over that of the source, depends on the spectrum of the column that feeds the streak, so a mock and
a real streak are each compared with the ratio expected from their own column
(`measure_oot_streak.expected_far_hardness`: every event of the column given copies on the far-side rows with the
table's factor; for real streaks the events come from the PPS event lists).  The 78 real Full Frame streaks read
0.027 +- 0.017 below their expectation, 42 realistic TNG views 0.026 +- 0.026 (median) below theirs: the estimator
reads about 0.03 low on both, and the shift itself follows the calibration.  The real streaks give 0.83
(0.71-0.96), the TNG views 0.86 with this table, the remaining 0.01-0.02 being the TNG columns, which are slightly
harder against their cores than the real ones.  The fixture view of the e2e test has a softer source (HR 0.76
against 1.07 for the real streaks) and reads 0.89 +- 0.05 over 12 seeds against 0.90 expected from its own column;
one seed scatters by 0.16, and the shift against the same seed with unchanged energies by 0.06 about its
expectation (-0.13), so the e2e test compares its run with the expectation from its column and tests the paired
shift, not the real range.

## Choices that are kept, with the reason

* The FWC event density and the CCF soft-proton maps are placed on the packaged detector mask by SAS's own DET -> sky
  solution of the observation the masks were made from (0827340901), read event by event from its PPS event lists
  (`donor_background.DET_TO_MASK`, `tools/det_to_mask.py`; scatter 0.12 arcsec).  Until 2026-11-03 a registration
  cross-correlated with the mask did this; the two agree to one mask pixel.
* `donor_tables.fwc.band_conv`, the ratio of the stage's FWC spectrum to the CCF FWC event rate per band: both are
  calibration data, so it is a unit conversion.
* Selection thresholds that classify rather than scale (anomalous MOS CCDs, footprint, matching costs).
