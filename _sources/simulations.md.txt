# Simulations and the ingest stage

The pipeline observes clusters from five simulations: TNG-Cluster (with the TNG300, TNG100 and TNG50 boxes),
FLAMINGO, SIMBA and EAGLE.  TNG-Cluster and FLAMINGO L1_m8 are the production pair.  SIMBA and EAGLE are internal
validation runs (see "Data use" below).

## One graph for every simulation

Only the `ingest` stage reads a simulation's own files.  Each simulation is described by a YAML descriptor
(`src/tngxmm/ingest/descriptors/<name>.yml`): cosmology, snapshots and redshifts, halo catalogue and centre, the
conversion of every native field to the canonical one, and the rules for star-forming, wind and net-heating gas.
A preset that names a descriptor (`sim.source`) carries its hash, so a changed descriptor changes the spec hash.

The stage dispatches a planned run to the loader of its simulation (`tngxmm.ingest.loaders`):

| loader | simulations | route |
|---|---|---|
| `ingest.tng` | TNG-Cluster, TNG300, TNG100, TNG50 | the snapshot read through the cube index files of the mock-cutout stage, converted to canonical cells in memory |
| `ingest.simba_remote`, `ingest.eagle_remote` | SIMBA m100n1024, EAGLE Ref-L0100N1504 | canonical cubes streamed from the original snapshots (SIMBA by HTTP ranges from the SIMBA team's server, EAGLE from the hdfstream service), checked against their manifests; the TNG-format conversions on Vera supply the halo catalogue only (descriptors `simba_tngformat`, `eagle_tngformat`) |
| `ingest.swift` | FLAMINGO L1_m10 (local copy) | canonical cubes written from the SWIFT snapshots and SOAP catalogues |
| `ingest.swift_remote` | FLAMINGO L1_m8 | canonical cubes streamed from the FLAMINGO data service; the Kedro node checks them against their manifests |

After the stage every simulation passes through the same nodes with the same inputs and outputs: the preset enters
the graph only as a parameter.  `tests/unit/test_single_graph.py` builds the Kedro pipelines under the presets `v1`,
`v1_tng300`, `v1_flamingo`, `v1_flamingo_l1m8`, `v1_simba` and `v1_eagle` and asserts that every node (name, inputs,
outputs, function, tags) is identical, and that no module outside `tngxmm.ingest` branches on the simulation or its
family, apart from the pinned list of TNG target-list builders.  What still differs is data, not graph: the
generation reads materialised cubes for FLAMINGO, SIMBA and EAGLE (`runtime.cube_store = 'canonical'`) and the index
files for TNG (`'index'`).

The mocks of each simulation are made in its own cosmology: the luminosity and angular-diameter distances at z_obs
and the critical density of R500c and of the hydrostatic truth come from the simulation's descriptor (for TNG, the
preset's cosmology, which is TNG's own).  Every fit, mock
and real, uses one analysis cosmology, Planck 2015 as TNG adopts it (H0 = 67.74 km/s/Mpc, Omega_m = 0.3089; ledger
`multisim.generation_cosmology`).  A FLAMINGO view (H0 = 68.1 km/s/Mpc, Omega_m = 0.306) is therefore fitted in a
cosmology other than its own: the angular scale differs by about 0.5 per cent at low z, the fitted mass by at most
0.01 dex (measured in the L_X gap investigation; STATUS F6).

## Emission abundances

The emission uses the element abundances of the gas (`sim.emission_abundances`):

* `full` (the default of every production preset since 2026-10-28): every element the simulation tracks, per cell;
  helium from the simulation; the untracked metals at solar ratios scaled by the remaining metal mass
  (`sim.emission_untracked = 'other-solar'`; Lodders 2003, Table 4);
* `common`: the total metallicity with solar ratios for every simulation (preset `v1_common`, the only mode that
  reads the TNG mini snapshots, which carry no element fractions);
* unset: the legacy table of release 1.1 (AG89 helium, metals scaled by total Z).  It made TNG-Cluster 0.022 dex
  brighter than `full` does, FLAMINGO 0.005 dex.

The ledger entries are `multisim.abundance_modes`, `multisim.helium`, `multisim.apec_elements` and
`multisim.tracked_elements` ({doc}`assumptions`).

## FLAMINGO L1_m8

L1_m8 (L1000N3600, 3600^3 gas particles of 1.3e8 Msun) is the highest-resolution hydrodynamical run
of FLAMINGO (Schaye et al. 2023).  It is read from the public FLAMINGO data service (Helly et al. 2026) with the
`hdfstream` client, which returns any slice or index list of a remote HDF5 dataset and decodes the lossy SWIFT
compression on the server.  The service is reachable from the login node only.  Snapshots 78, 76, 74, 72, 70, 68,
63, 58 and 48 (z = 0 to 1.5); halos from SOAP-HBT, centrals only, centred on the most bound particle, with the HBT+
track id for the identity across snapshots.  The local L1000N0900 copy (L1_m10, preset `v1_flamingo`) is kept as
the low-resolution comparison.

The sample (`internal/ledger_tests/multisim/l1m8_sample.py`, seed 20261028; the rule was written before the
streaming) fills the (mass, redshift) plane of the 638 real observations with about 5,000
halo-snapshots:

* each real observation is assigned to the nearest of the nine snapshots; its candidates are the halos there with
  log10 M500c(true) between log M_cat - 0.15 and log M_cat + 0.25 (hydrostatic fit masses are 0.75-0.9 of the true
  mass, so the window is centred above the catalogue mass; it decides only which halos are streamed);
* every central above 6e14 Msun is kept at every snapshot;
* below that, in cells of 0.1 dex in mass from 1.2e14 Msun and one snapshot, a random subsample of
  min(available, max(8, f w)) halos, w the density of the real pool in the cell and f = 13.9 set so that the total
  is 5,000;
* halos whose track is not yet in the sample at another snapshot are drawn first.

The result is 5,000 halo-snapshots on 2,143 distinct tracks.  The download finished on 2026-10-30.

`python -m tngxmm.ingest.swift_remote soap` writes the SOAP tables; `... cubes` streams each halo's
particles and writes one HDF5 file per snapshot, `<cube_root>/flamingo_l1m8/cubes_<snap:03d>.h5`, with a
`cubes_<snap:03d>.manifest.json` beside it.  Each cube holds every particle (gas, dark matter, stars, black holes)
in the axis-aligned cube of half-side max(5.66 R500c, 1.001 x 3 R200c) about the halo centre, in physical units,
with a sha256 per dataset.  5.66 = 4 sqrt(2): a cylinder of radius and half-length 4 R500c fits inside this cube
along any line of sight, which the icosahedral projections of release 1.2 need; the 3 R200c term keeps the truth
sphere inside the cube for the few halos with R200c / R500c > 1.89.  The file records the remote snapshot, the SOAP
path and its version, and FLAMINGO's own per-particle 0.5-2 keV luminosity summed inside R500c as a check of our
emission (ours is +0.028 dex higher, the APEC against Cloudy offset).  Halos are read in Morton order of their
top-level cell, one request at a time; a cell's coordinates are read once and every other field only for the
particles inside some cube.

Streaming the whole sample took about 35 hours on the login node, so it is a documented pre-step
(`tngxmm_dev/MULTISIM/l1m8/run_sample.sh`), not a node.  The node `ingest.build_canonical_cubes` then opens the
cube files read-only and checks every planned halo against the manifest: present, complete, particle counts and
stored sha256 per dataset (`ingest.verify_deep` also rehashes the data).  It returns the same `canonical_cubes`
record as the other loaders, and the post-ingest nodes are those of TNG-Cluster.  The check takes about 0.7 s per
halo (snapshot 78: 665 halos, no failure).  Away from the cluster, `ingest.stream_missing=true` makes the node
fetch the planned halos that the cube file lacks before it checks them (about 25 s and 440 MB of transfer per halo),
so the cubes need not be copied: `kedro run --pipeline ingest --params
preset=v1_flamingo_l1m8,ingest.cube_root=<dir>,ingest.stream_missing=true`.

`tngxmm donors sim-targets` builds the list of any simulation stored as canonical cubes from the
cube manifests, with the TNG-Cluster split and donor rules (`tngxmm.donors.build_sim_targets`).  For L1_m8 the
object is the HBT+ track:

    tngxmm donors sim-targets --preset v1_flamingo_l1m8 --cube-root <cube_root> --identity track_id \
        --replica campaigns/v4/targets_v4.csv --coverage campaigns/v4/replica_coverage.csv \
        --out-dir campaigns/v4_flamingo_l1m8

It gives 15,003 views of 5,001 halo-snapshots on 2,145 tracks; four halos at z = 1.0 and 1.5 have SOAP indices too
long for the mock obsid and get no view (`campaigns/v4_flamingo_l1m8/README.md`).

L1_m8 is not a resolution-converged version of L1_m10 in L_X.  At the same true M500c and z = 0.2,
L1_m8 is fainter than L1_m10 by 0.13-0.17 dex at 4-10e14 Msun and brighter by 0.1 dex at 1-1.6e14 Msun; the gas
fractions account for 0.04-0.06 dex of the difference at the high-mass end (SOAP luminosities in the R500c sphere,
3,285 L1_m8 centrals; `LXGAP_INVESTIGATION/REPORT.md`, section 5).  Comparisons of TNG-Cluster with FLAMINGO in
this documentation use L1_m8.

## The L_X difference between TNG-Cluster and FLAMINGO

At catalogue level TNG-Cluster is 0.2-0.4 dex brighter in 0.5-2 keV than the observed L_X-M relations at fixed true
mass, and FLAMINGO lies close to them ({doc}`dev/LX_CROSS_SIM`).  A pair test asked whether the same difference
appears through our own mock pipeline (DECISIONS, "L_X gap test", job 1890897; the reading rule was fixed before
the job).

The test has 199 TNG-Cluster views at z = 0.1-0.5, as many per snapshot as the real pool has at that redshift, each
paired with the L1_m8 halo of the matching snapshot nearest in true M500c (median |dlog M| 0.001 dex); within a pair
the same donor observation, redshift, line of sight and roll, the same instrument and background code, `full`
abundances, 5.66 R500c cubes.

The result (median log10 L_FLAMINGO / L_TNG, 95 per cent bootstrap interval over pairs):

| quantity | all pairs | 1-2e14 Msun | 2-4e14 Msun | above 4e14 Msun |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L_X(1 Mpc), photometry of the mocks | -0.325 [-0.375, -0.292] (193) | -0.244 | -0.323 | -0.391 |
| intrinsic, R500c cylinder | -0.351 [-0.379, -0.305] (199) | -0.278 | -0.330 | -0.393 |

The photometry reproduces the intrinsic difference to 0.03 dex.  In the median the measurement reads both arms low:
the median of log10(measured / intrinsic L_X(1 Mpc)), the net ratio of the measurement chain, is -0.052 dex for
TNG-Cluster and -0.041 dex for FLAMINGO (193 views each; on the v1.0.1 trees, an earlier configuration, -0.071 dex
over 6,967 TNG-Cluster views).  Against the real pool (each mock against the median real L_X(1 Mpc) within 0.1 dex in
catalogue mass and 0.1 in z; different mass definitions, no conversion): FLAMINGO L1_m8 -0.04 dex on true M500c
and +0.03 dex on its 3-D hydrostatic mass; TNG-Cluster +0.31 and +0.41 dex.  The ratio is not known for the real
clusters, so whether it cancels in these offsets is not measured ({doc}`definitions`, L_X).

An independent recomputation of the intrinsic luminosities of the same pairs gives
-0.36 dex, and the catalogue-level comparison of temet's luminosities of TNG-Cluster with FLAMINGO's SOAP
luminosities at matched mass gives -0.42 dex.  About 60 per cent of the difference comes from the gas fraction, the
rest from the structure of the gas (the emission measure of the 0.15-1 R500c shell beyond what the gas fraction
gives).  An audit of every step of the emission, projection, photometry and mass axis found no step that raises the
TNG-Cluster luminosities by more than 0.022 dex (the legacy abundance table, now replaced by `full`).

The excess is a property of the TNG-Cluster gas.  The same pipeline puts FLAMINGO on the observed
relation, so the pipeline does not create it, and no defensible change of the emission model removes it
({doc}`dev/LX_GAS_EXPLORATION`: physically defensible departures remove at most about 0.06 dex).  The excess is
reported as a model property and is not calibrated away.

## Data use

SIMBA and EAGLE are read from the particle data their teams released (since 2026-11-01); the TNG-format conversions on
Vera still supply their halo catalogues.  The readme of the conversions asks users to contact their author before
use, which has not been done, so both stay internal validation runs as they stand.  SIMBA's decoupled wind particles
are identified in the original snapshots (DelayTime > 0) and do not emit (DECISIONS "P2b").  FLAMINGO L1_m8 and
L1_m10 are public data.
